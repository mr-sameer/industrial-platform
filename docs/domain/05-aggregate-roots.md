# 5. Aggregate Roots

An aggregate root is the single entity through which all writes to a
cluster of related entities must pass, so that the cluster's invariants
(business rules that must hold *within* one transaction) are enforced in
one place. Anything inside an aggregate can be loaded/modified together;
anything outside must be referenced by id and modified through its own
aggregate root — never reached into directly.

## Identified aggregate roots

### Company (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `Company` itself, `CompanyMember`, `Factory`, `Gallery`
(when owned by Company/Factory), general `Document`s.

**Transactional boundary:** adding/removing a `CompanyMember`, adding a
`Factory`, or changing `Company` status must be atomic with the "exactly
one Owner" invariant (Section 8) — e.g. removing the last Owner and
failing to promote a new one in the same transaction would leave the
aggregate invalid. `Product` and `Certificate`/`Verification` are
**deliberately excluded** from this aggregate (see their own roots
below) — a Company changing its name should never need to lock or
reload its entire product catalog.

**Does not contain:** `Product` (own aggregate — high write frequency,
independent lifecycle), `Verification`/`Certificate` (own aggregate —
different actor writes to it, see below), `Review` (authored by a
different actor entirely — a Company cannot write to its own reviews).

---

### Product (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `Product` itself, `ProductVariant`, `Gallery` (when owned
by Product).

**Transactional boundary:** adding/removing/reordering `ProductVariant`s
and updating the parent `Product`'s status happen together — e.g.
archiving a Product should be able to cascade-consider its Variants in
one transaction. References `Company` (via company_id) and `Category`
(via category_id) by id only — a Product update never needs to load or
lock the parent Company aggregate.

**Why its own root, not part of Company:** products are updated far more
frequently and independently than company profile data (a supplier
adding a new SKU shouldn't contend for a lock with, say, another admin
updating the company's headquarters address). Splitting them keeps write
throughput on each independent.

---

### Verification (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `Verification` itself, and — for the specific transaction
of deciding a verification — the `Certificate` records it evaluated
(read/referenced, with their `verification_status` field written as part
of the same transaction as the Verification decision).

**Transactional boundary:** approving a Verification and marking its
considered Certificates as `verified` must be atomic — a crash between
those two writes would leave the system in an inconsistent state (a
"verified" company with certificates still showing `pending`). References
`Company` by id only.

**Why its own root, not part of Company:** the actor writing to this
aggregate (Platform Admin/Moderator) is different from the actor writing
to the Company aggregate (Company Owner/Admin) — separating them means
the authorization check for "can write to this aggregate" is simple and
uniform within each root, rather than a single Company aggregate needing
mixed-authority write rules depending on *which field* is being changed.

---

### User (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `User` itself (already Module 2/2.5's boundary — `Session`,
`RefreshToken`, password history, etc. are already correctly scoped to
this aggregate and unchanged by this document).

**Transactional boundary:** unchanged from the existing implementation.
`CompanyMember` rows referencing this User are **not** part of this
aggregate (they belong to the `Company` aggregate) — a User aggregate
never needs to load every company it's a member of just to, say, change
its email address.

---

### AI Conversation (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `AIConversation` itself, its message/turn history, and
`AIRecommendation`s generated *within* that conversation.

**Transactional boundary:** appending a new turn and any
`AIRecommendation` it produces should commit together — a partially
recorded conversation turn (prompt saved, response lost) is a real
failure mode worth preventing at the aggregate-boundary level. References
`User` by id only; references `Company`/`Product` (what a recommendation
is *about*) by id only, never loading those aggregates into the same
transaction.

**Why its own root:** AI-generated content has a fundamentally different
write pattern (append-only, high volume, generated by a system actor)
than every other aggregate here — isolating it means an AI-throughput
problem never contends with, say, Company or Product write locks.

---

### Collection (aggregate root)
**Contains:** `Collection` itself and its `SavedSupplier`/Product
reference list.

**Transactional boundary:** adding/removing items from a Collection is
atomic with the Collection's own metadata (e.g. renaming). References
`Company`/`Product` by id only.

**Why its own root, and why `SavedSupplier` isn't independently a root:**
a `SavedSupplier` not attached to any Collection still needs to exist
(the brief lists it as its own entity, and "save without organizing" is
a valid simpler action than "save into a collection") — so `SavedSupplier`
is better understood as **usually** owned by a `Collection` aggregate but
**capable of existing as its own minimal aggregate** when
`collection_id` is null. This is a deliberate, slightly unusual call —
see Section 18 for the alternative considered (making SavedSupplier
always its own root, with Collection just a tag) and why the chosen
design was preferred (it keeps "everything in my Q3 RFQ collection" a
single atomic read/write unit, which is the more common access pattern).

---

## Aggregate root summary table

| Aggregate Root | Contains | Excludes (referenced by id only) | Primary writer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Company` | CompanyMember, Factory, Gallery*, Document | Product, Verification, Certificate, Review | Company Owner/Admin |
| `Product` | ProductVariant, Gallery* | Company (parent), Reviews | Company Owner/Admin/Editor |
| `Verification` | (evaluates) Certificate.verification_status | Company (parent) | Platform Admin/Moderator |
| `User` | Session, RefreshToken, password history *(existing, unchanged)* | CompanyMember, Review, SavedSupplier | The User themself |
| `AIConversation` | Turns, AIRecommendation (in-conversation) | Company, Product (referenced only) | AI system, on behalf of User |
| `Collection` | SavedSupplier (when grouped), Product references | Company, Product (referenced only) | The owning User |

*`Gallery` belongs to whichever of Company/Factory/Product it's attached
to — it is never its own independent aggregate.

## What is explicitly NOT an aggregate root

`Review`, `Certificate`, `Notification`, `AuditLog`, `SearchQuery`,
`SearchHistory`, `Activity` are all **entities within** an aggregate (or
simple standalone records with no internal invariants complex enough to
need aggregate-root treatment) rather than aggregate roots themselves —
each is written by exactly one clear actor into exactly one place, with
no multi-entity invariant that needs transactional protection beyond
normal single-row consistency.
